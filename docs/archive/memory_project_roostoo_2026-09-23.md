Roostoo hackathon research repo at `~/roostoo-hackathon` (git, local-only). Live window 2026-10-04 to 2026-10-17, $100k, 1x, directional only. Two gates: Screen 2 ranks on **raw return** (top 20 per region advance), Screen 3 then ranks survivors on `0.4*Sortino + 0.3*Sharpe + 0.3*Calmar`. Read `DECISIONS.md` first - every non-obvious choice is anchored there and the repo forbids code comments.

Key standing results as of 2026-09-19 (trial ledger at 167):

- **BTC buy-and-hold beat every constructed strategy for five stages**, and it is the only one with an honest trial count of 1. Gate 4 failed for all 131 searched configurations.
- **Zarattini/Pagani/Barbon (SSRN 5209907) Donchian ensemble finally beats it on Screen 3**: nine lookbacks 5/10/20/30/60/90/150/250/360d, ratcheting **channel-midpoint** stop (not ATR), equal-weight, 25% vol target on 90d realised vol, gross capped 1.0, monthly top-20 snapshot. On BTC alone with portfolio-level vol targeting: OOS Screen 3 1.632 vs hold 1.364; post-publication 0.667 vs 0.150. DSR 0.9938 at N=1, 0.9719 at N=2 - **the first Gate 4 pass in the repo** - but 0.574 at the 27 sizing readings actually tried, so the pass is contingent on declaring one reading prospectively.
- **The 14-day Screen 3 composite is a sign indicator, not a skill measure.** Every configuration with a negative median 14-day return scores about -2.7 to -3.9; BTC hold scores +3.72. The capped Calmar term pins at its floor. So the quantity that actually predicts Screen 3 rank is P(fortnight finishes positive) - and on that BTC hold wins 55.5%/29.8% (P>0 / P>5%) against 45.2%/16.8% for the best ensemble. **Optimising Screen 3 reduces the chance of ever clearing Screen 2.** Unresolved.
- **Tested on the competition's actual shape (2026-09-19) the ensemble FAILS.** Scoring every contiguous 14-day OOS window and pairing it against BTC hold *over the same fourteen days*: the candidate beats hold on return in 34.3% of 1345 windows, on Screen 3 in **10.3%**, and clears Screen 2 *and* beats hold on Screen 3 in **0.2%**. Nothing tested exceeds 5.2% on that joint measure. The 1.632-vs-1.364 Screen 3 win is a nine-year compounding statistic that does not exist at 14-day resolution.
- **The two screens are collinear at 14 days, not merely in tension.** Across a permanent-BTC-core/conviction-sleeve range the composite rises monotonically as qualification probability falls (1.385 @ 26.0%, 1.393 @ 23.3%, 1.632 @ 17.2%) with no interior point where both improve. Downside deviation and the right tail are the same beta. This refutes plan Section 1's claim that asymmetry serves both screens.
- Entry-conviction conditioning is rejected: monotonic 2023-2025 (P>5% of 24.9% at high conviction vs 15.6% at low), inverted post-publication on 25 windows.
- Gotchas: "leverage capped at 1.0" must cap **gross exposure**, not the vol scalar (capping the scalar pinned the book at 18.5% invested). The repo's original `donchian_breakout` is a **one-bar pulse, not a channel rule** (8.8% mean gross exposure) so the old family comparison's verdict on breakout is void. ATR stops destroy trend returns here; the midpoint ratchet is what produces the +1.35 skew. Panel starts 2017-08-17 (Binance inception) so the paper's 2015 start is not reproducible.
- Roostoo mirrors Binance **exactly** (median 0.00 bps deviation), so Binance klines are the same series, not a proxy. Shorting is live via `/v6/short_open|short_close|short_positions` but costs 0.1% both legs with no maker discount, so it is a hedge not a symmetric alpha source.

**2026-09-20: Gate 4 is settled as unrecoverable. Stop spending research on it.**
The open question was whether the honest Deflated Sharpe denominator was the raw ledger (754) or a smaller count of genuinely independent selection decisions.
A counting rule was written and committed **before** the number was computed (that ordering is the whole point; a rule chosen after seeing which number helps is not a correction) and applied uniformly: `n_trials` enters `expected_max_sharpe` as "the number of candidates whose MAXIMUM is reported", not as compute spent, so rows that state they were never candidates, re-evaluations of an already-counted config, and rows scored on a different objective form different families.
Result: 754 -> **470** Sharpe-family, DSR 0.8009 -> **0.8445** against a required 0.95. Still fails.
Passing needs N<=94 (unreachable honestly), **3,579 daily obs / 9.8 years** against 1,358 held, or **annual Sharpe 2.498** against 2.206 measured. Confirming fees moves Sharpe ~0.11 per 5 bps, an order too small.
The rule is executable at `core.config.trial_counts_by_family` and the gate artifact now always reports both numbers. Written up at `DECISIONS.md#trial-counting-rule`.
**Practical consequence: the marginal DSR cost of a sweep is no longer the reason not to run one. The reason is that seven families in a row returned nulls.**

**2026-09-20 literature review (`RESEARCH_SHORT_HORIZON.md`), screened on one question: does per-trade edge clear a 10bps round trip?**
**arXiv:2608.21888 independently reproduces this repo's scalp null at 183 Binance pairs**: 15-min reversal is real (90% of pairs, permutation null, frozen holdout) but gross edge peaks at **1.3bps vs 5bps round trip**, median pair 0.46bps, *thresholding makes it worse* ("trading fewer, better bars shrinks opportunity count faster than it grows the edge" - verbatim the repo's own entry_z finding), and 5-min is worse still (0.15bps). **Never rebuild a sub-hourly family; the edge is a property of the horizon.** arXiv:2606.00060 (hourly BTC ML, 27-fold walk-forward) agrees: naive sign rules give -64% ARC at 10bps, and even cost-aware ones don't beat buy-and-hold after bootstrap adjustment.
Two mechanisms tested: (1) **cost-aware rotation filter = NULL** - `lambda*10bps` is trivial vs 40-bar momentum dispersion so turnover falls only 1-6% and effects are 4-10x below the repo's own 0.027 noise threshold; the nonsense control (random blocking of the same fraction) went the *wrong* way, confirming the sign but not the size. (2) **passive-fill adverse selection is a REAL gap but bounded**: `portfolio/backtest.py` sets `crossing = 0.0` for LIMIT, so every backtest prices a passive fill at fee + ZERO spread with 100% fill, while arXiv:2502.18625 shows makers fill with probability 1 exactly when the next move is against them. Bracketing fee-only vs fee+half-spread costs donchian_4h only **0.042 Sharpe** (mean half-spread 4.17bps, amortised over multi-day holds). Fee confirmation is worth ~3x more, so priorities unchanged - **but this inverts at hourly holds**, another reason not to shorten the clock.

**Operational gotchas found 2026-09-20:** a `pgrep` guard that cannot match its own process is not a guard (`$ROOT` arrived as a TRAILING `bash -c` arg and the worker argv had no path, so two scanners ran concurrently on one state store); a default argument pointing at a fixed path goes stale exactly like a hardcoded list (`bot.paper_lab --report` silently described v1); a **frozen book's telemetry is indistinguishable from a live one's** unless age since last cycle is shown; and the paper lab ran unsupervised although its only product is elapsed time against a 28-day minimum.
`CLAUDE.md` named `config/bot_a_4h.yaml` long after the rename to `donchian_4h` deleted it.

**2026-09-21 literature review (`RESEARCH_PORTFOLIO_RETURN.md`), on the RANK objective rather than on signals.**
**arXiv:2412.04490 (Stanek, M6 Investment Challenge) is the closest published analogue.** Two results: extreme leaderboard Sharpes are within chance for the field size (so never revise strategy on a mid-competition leaderboard reading, ours or a rival's); and the rank-optimal policy is **adversarial and rank-conditional** - diverge from what the FIELD holds when behind, mimic it when ahead. Confirmed empirically in the M6 weights, and by Brown/Harlow/Starks 1996 (mid-year losers raise volatility). **This reframes the BTC floor**: in a crypto contest BTC is what the field holds, so a constant floor is the mimic-the-crowd move and is right only while already ABOVE the cut. The repo's floor table measures it against the MARKET, which is not what a rank is scored against. Added **question 7 to `ORGANISER_QUESTIONS.md`: is a live leaderboard visible during the window?** - without it the rank-conditional policy is unimplementable and the floor stays off.
Supporting: Browne 1999 (max P(target by deadline) = digital option on the GROWTH-optimal portfolio, so aggression belongs in sizing not selection - which is why top1 raised P(>20%) while going negative); arXiv:2604.17577 finite-horizon quantile Kelly (short horizon licenses overbetting relative to Kelly, but the licence is bounded - top1 is what overshooting looks like); Liu/Tsyvinski/Wu 2022 JF (size long-short significantly NEGATIVE, i.e. large caps win - first outside support for the top-30-by-ADV universe rule). **The MAX/lottery literature contradicts itself on sign - do NOT tilt toward explosive coins; buy the right tail through concentration on a validated signal.**
